The friction reads as cultural. The cost lands on the P&L.

01

Decisions keep coming back.

Agreement in the meeting produces no action after it. The same decision is taken three times and executed once.

02

Responsibilities are read differently.

03

Feedback changes nothing.

Delivered, received, acknowledged — and the behaviour stays as it was. The correction arrives weeks later, filed as a technical error.

04

02 / WHAT IT COSTS

Management practice is measurable. So is the gap between two of them.

Research on management practice across tens of thousands of establishments has established something useful here: how a firm runs its targets, its monitoring and its people is not soft, and the differences between firms explain a substantial share of the differences in productivity between them.
When one operation contains two management traditions, the organisation is carrying two systems at once and paying for the interference between them.

THE UPSIDE

Worked deliberately, the same difference becomes an advantage. Evidence on international teams points the same way: diversity of background raises output when the team has the means to integrate it, and depresses it when it does not. The variable is not who is in the room. It is whether the operation has built the mechanism to convert two perspectives into one decision. That mechanism is what this work installs.

Intercultural Diagnostic Report — sample

Component manufacturer · 62 employees · head office in Suzhou

14 respondents · 3 departments · 18-day window

FINDING 02 — Confirmation of understanding between head office and local operation

On the statement “When I speak with colleagues from the other country, I can confirm that I was understood”, respondents from head office averaged 4.3. Locally hired respondents averaged 2.2 on the same statement. The 2.1-point gap is the widest in the set.

The reading is not language proficiency: 9 of 14 respondents work in English and report being comfortable in it. What the open answers show is asymmetry of checking. Three local accounts describe an instruction received by message, then executed, then corrected after delivery. No head-office account mentions the same cycle — from that side, the instruction was given and the task was completed.

This is a documented pattern in international teams: the sender treats silence as agreement, and the receiver treats a clarifying question as an admission of incompetence. The rework surfaces weeks later and is recorded as a technical error.

What this company already does well: three respondents independently named the directors' openness to conversation outside the hierarchy. The channel exists. It is not being used to confirm tasks.

Recommended action: a two-line instruction read-back protocol, applied in the technical department for 30 days, with a closing review. Near-zero operating cost. The verification indicator is the rework count over the period.
